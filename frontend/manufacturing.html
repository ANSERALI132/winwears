Manufacturing

From factory floor to final match.

Eight stages between a roll of material and a packed carton. This is the sequence every WIN WEARS football goes through.

The facility

Our own factory.
Our own people.

A production line built specifically around footballs — not a general sporting-goods floor that also makes balls.

The process

Eight stages, in order.

Each stage below describes our general production capability. Exact methods and materials are agreed per order and confirmed at sampling.

01

Material Selection

Outer material, lining and bladder are chosen against how the ball will actually be used — match, training, promotional. Thickness, grain and backing all change how the finished ball feels and how long it lasts.

PUTPULining clothButyl / latex
02

Panel Preparation

Sheets are laminated with lining, then cut to the panel shapes for the chosen layout. Consistent panel dimensions are what make a ball round later — a millimetre out here shows up as a wobble in flight.

LaminationDie cutting32 / low panel / 20 panel
03

Printing

Colourways, club crests, sponsor marks and full panel graphics are applied before assembly, while the panels are still flat. Artwork is proofed with you before a run is printed.

Screen printHeat transferArtwork proofing
04

Stitching & Bonding

Hand stitching for match ranges where seam strength matters, machine stitching where consistency at volume matters, and thermal bonding where a seamless surface is the point. The method follows the specification, not the other way round.

Hand stitchedMachine stitchedThermal bonded
05

Bladder Installation

The bladder goes in and the valve is seated and checked. Butyl holds pressure longer between sessions; latex gives a livelier rebound. Which one you want depends on whether the ball is inflated weekly or daily.

ButylLatexValve seating
06

Shape & Pressure Inspection

Balls are inflated and checked for roundness, circumference, weight and pressure retention. Anything outside tolerance goes back rather than forward.

SphericityCircumferenceWeightAir retention
07

Quality Control

Seam integrity, print registration, colour match against the approved proof, and surface finish. This is the last point at which a problem is cheap to fix, so it is where we spend the time.

Seam checkPrint registrationColour match
08

Final Packaging

Deflated or inflated, boxed, netted or bagged, with your printed packaging and cartons ready for export. Packing format is agreed with you before production finishes.

Printed boxesNets & hang tagsExport cartons
