Section 03 — Ball Technology

What goes into the ball.

Drag the ball to turn it. Every layer below is a decision we make with you at order stage — not a fixed recipe.

PU for match ranges, TPU where durability outweighs touch. Grain, thickness and gloss are all specified per order, and we will sample before a run is committed.

Butyl for air retention, latex for rebound feel. The choice changes how the ball holds pressure between sessions, so we match it to how the ball will actually be used.

From the traditional 32-panel layout to low-panel and 20-panel thermal builds. Panel count and shape drive both the flight character and how much artwork area you have to work with.

Embossed grain, dimpled or smooth. Texture affects grip in wet conditions and how the print sits on the panel — we will show you samples side by side.

Hand stitched for seam strength, machine stitched for consistency at volume. Thread type and stitch density are set per range.

Heat and pressure instead of thread. No stitch channels means a more uniform surface and less water taken on through the seams during play.

Built for volume use — academy sessions, promotional runs and events. Harder wearing than a match PU, and more forgiving of rough surfaces.

A function of sphericity, panel symmetry and surface. We check roundness and balance on finished balls rather than assuming the build got it right.

Seam integrity, material thickness and bladder retention together decide how long a ball lasts. Tell us the surface it will live on and we will spec accordingly.
